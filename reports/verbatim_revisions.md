# Verbatim transcription: what had to be corrected

Generated by `scripts/compile_verbatim_revisions.py`. Diffs are computed from the configs; the **issue** classification on each project is a reading of those diffs, not a field in the registry.

**7 of 9** canonical projects have a verbatim run at all (`emotion_regulation_2022` and `social` do not: ER's canonical family starts at v2, and social's verbatim is registered only for `annotation_only`).

**4 of 7** required revision before reaching `untuned`, and **3 of 7** of those revisions correct an actual defect in the transcription rather than adding specificity.

## What the configs cannot tell you

Disclosed by the author on 2026-11-03, and recorded here because no config captures it.

In some of the runs registered as `untuned`, the criteria **were** revised after looking at reports, at the **screening stage**, typically after reading fewer than five papers. Those revisions were manually reviewed and found consistent with the source report -- they corrected an **omission** or an **under-specification** in it, rather than fitting the benchmark's answers. The benchmark acted as a small set of test cases that exposed the defect; it was not the target.

So the defensible claim is **"not systematically fitted to benchmark error reports"**, not "no benchmark output was ever consulted". The second would be false for several projects. Which ones is not recorded in the repo and is being reconstructed from memory; until that list exists, this document cannot name them, and any wording in the manuscript should avoid implying the stronger claim.

Two projects have **no verbatim run at all**: `emotion_regulation_2022` and `social`. `verbatim` is deliberately left null for them rather than standing in the lowest-numbered version, which would read as a transcribe-first run it is not -- in both cases the researcher read the criteria, saw obvious errors and corrected them on the first attempt instead of running the faithful version. ER's v2 header lists changes vs v1 that "each correct a clear misreading or an outright omission of a stated criterion".

They are therefore excluded from the denominator, which is why it is **7**, not 9. Note this makes the rate below if anything CONSERVATIVE: both excluded projects did need corrections, but made them before the first run, so they produce no diff to count.

## cue_reactivity  (`v1` → `v4`)

**Issue: invented restriction.** v1 excluded non-empirical papers "without original fMRI data IN HEALTHY ADULTS", a restriction the source paper never states. v2's own header records it as an error present in two places (full-text exclusion and annotation inclusion). The annotation scope was also narrowed to "drug or natural reward" where the paper's construct is reward-related cue reactivity generally.

*full-text screening — excl*

- `REMOVED` Non-empirical papers (e.g., reviews, meta-analyses, methods-only, simulations) without original fMRI data in healthy adults.
- `ADDED` Non-empirical papers (e.g., reviews, meta-analyses, methods-only, simulations) without original human fMRI data.

*annotation — incl*

- `REMOVED` Whole-brain evidence- The analysis must be from a group-level, univariate whole-brain (voxelwise) task-evoked statistical map (e.g., main effect or within-group contrast) that is from the healthy/control group and is generalizable to the healthy adult population.
- `REMOVED` Contrast or analysis must be relevant to drug or natural reward-related cue-reactivity using visual stimuli (e.g., drug cues>neutral cues, sexual cues>neutral cues, food cues>neutral cues).
- `ADDED` Whole-brain evidence- The analysis must be from a group-level, univariate whole-brain (voxelwise) task-evoked statistical map (e.g., main effect or within-group contrast). Between-group contrasts or interactions (e.g., subgroup vs subgroup, patient vs control) are acceptable if they compare the target within group contrast to another group (e.g., patient drug cues>neutral cues vs control drug cues>neutral cues).
- `ADDED` Contrast or analysis must be relevant to reward-related cue-reactivity using visual stimuli.

*annotation — excl*

- `REMOVED` Between-group contrasts or interactions (e.g., subgroup vs subgroup, patient vs control)

## decision_making  (`v1` → `v2`)

**Issue: under-specification, not error.** The only revision that is NOT a correction. v1's criteria were not wrong; they were silent on how to resolve ambiguity, so four inclusion criteria were ADDED and none removed: how to read a truncated analysis label, the paper's own subtype taxonomy, and study-level subtype mutual exclusivity. Counting this with the others would overstate the transcription-failure rate.

*annotation — incl*

- `ADDED` If an analysis label is generic/truncated (e.g., analysis_0), use table caption, neighboring contrasts, and study_fulltext to infer eligibility; do not fail only because the label text is sparse.
- `ADDED` Resolve subtype overlaps by primary uncertainty source- sensory evidence uncertainty -> perceptual_dm; uncertainty/ambiguity in contingencies or information -> ambiguous_dm; explicit known-risk vs safe/certain choice -> risky_dm.
- `ADDED` Study-level subtype mutual exclusivity is required- for each study/PMID, assign exactly one subtype among ambiguous_dm, perceptual_dm, and risky_dm.
- `ADDED` Once a study-level subtype is chosen, all included analyses from that study must follow that same subtype; do not split analyses from one study across multiple subtypes.

## dementia  (`v1` → `v3`)

**Issue: transcription error.** Three distinct defects. (1) A LOGICAL CONNECTIVE INVERTED: the sample rule read "at least 6 participants in EITHER the patient or healthy group", where the paper requires 6 in each -- corrected to "at least 6 in the patient group, and if there is a healthy/control group, at least 6 in the control group". (2) A PATIENT GROUP OMITTED: the paper includes Pick's disease; v1 admitted bvFTD only. (3) A CRITERION THAT CONTRADICTS THE DESIGN: v1 excluded "between-group contrasts or interactions", which is the contrast type this meta-analysis is built on.

*abstract screening — incl*

- `REMOVED` Study uses Voxel-Based Morphometry (VBM), fMRI (resting‐state or task based), or FDG‐PET as the imaging modality
- `REMOVED` Sample- participants included clinically diagnosed behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) patients
- `ADDED` Study uses Voxel-Based Morphometry (VBM), fMRI (resting‐state or task based), or FDG‐PET as the imaging modality to analyze whole brain
- `ADDED` Sample- participants included at least 6 human, clinically diagnosed behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) patients

*abstract screening — excl*

- `REMOVED` systematic reviews or meta-analyses
- `ADDED` Systematic reviews or meta-analyses
- `ADDED` Study should not focus on Parkinson's disease
- `ADDED` Study does not use neuroimaging
- `ADDED` studies with less than 6 participants

*full-text screening — incl*

- `REMOVED` Sample- participants included clinically diagnosed behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) patients
- `REMOVED` Sample- At least 6 participants in either the patient or healthy group
- `REMOVED` Studies that reported at least one result of a between‐group contrast in a defined stochastic space (i.e., Montreal Neurological Institute [MNI] or Talairach)
- `ADDED` Sample- participants included clinically diagnosed behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) or picks disease patients
- `ADDED` Sample- At least 6 participants in the patient group, and if there is a healthy/control group, at least 6 particpants in the control group
- `ADDED` Studies that reported at least one result of a between‐group contrast in a defined stochastic space (i.e., Montreal Neurological Institute [MNI] or Talairach) comparing bvFTD group to controls, where bvFTD group is not pooled with other patient groups in this result.

*annotation — incl*

- `REMOVED` uses Voxel-Based Morphometry, fMRI (resting‐state or task based), and FDG-PET (Fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography) as the imaging modality
- `REMOVED` Sample- participants included clinically diagnosed behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) patients with no concurrent psychiatric diagnosis (e.g., major depressive disorder and bipolar mood disorder), other forms of dementia or neurological symptoms, and no history of alcohol and substance abuse
- `REMOVED` Whole-brain evidence- The analysis must be from a group-level, whole-brain (voxelwise) map
- `ADDED` Whole-brain evidence- The analysis must be from a whole-brain (voxelwise) map
- `ADDED` Sample- analysis includes clinically diagnosed behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) or picks disease patients
- `ADDED` Analysis must compare the target bvFTD group to healthy controls

*annotation — excl*

- `REMOVED` Seed-based connectivity, effective connectivity (e.g., PPI, DCM), or resting-state analyses
- `REMOVED` Between-group contrasts or interactions (e.g., subgroup vs subgroup, patient vs control)
- `ADDED` Seed‐based functional connectivity (FC), diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), and cortical thickness methods
- `ADDED` Explicit evidence of pooling bvFTD group with other patient groups in the analysis (e.g. bvFTD + other dementia group vs controls)

## vbm_of_substance_use  (`v1` → `v2`)

**Issue: wrong modality encoded.** v1 required "original human fMRI data" in a VOXEL-BASED MORPHOMETRY meta-analysis, which is structural MRI. Also "cue type" as a reported variable, a leftover from the cue-reactivity config this one was templated from.

*full-text screening — incl*

- `REMOVED` Empirical English language MRI studies assessing grey matter (GM) volume differences between substance users and controls
- `REMOVED` Reporting of participant age/sex, cue type, MRI field strength, and preprocessing/statistical software is preferred; missing one or more of these details alone should not exclude an otherwise qualifying study.
- `ADDED` Empirical English language MRI studies assessing grey matter (GM) volume differences (e.g., volume, density, or concentration) between substance users and controls
- `ADDED` Reporting of participant age/sex, substance use characteristics, MRI field strength, and preprocessing/statistical software is preferred; missing one or more of these details alone should not exclude an otherwise qualifying study.

*full-text screening — excl*

- `REMOVED` Non-empirical papers (e.g., reviews, meta-analyses, methods-only, simulations) without original human fMRI data.
- `ADDED` Non-empirical papers (e.g., reviews, meta-analyses, methods-only, simulations) without original human structural MRI data.

*annotation — incl*

- `REMOVED` Grey matter volume comparison between substance users and controls, with a users < controls contrast indicating reduced GM in substance users.
- `REMOVED` Must be a voxelwise whole brain analysis
- `REMOVED` The inclusion of multiple experiments per article were only considered if the experiments overlapped in the control group, but not the substance user group. For example, moderate smoker<control, and separately severe smoker<control include two different samples of cigarette smokers.
- `ADDED` Voxelwise whole-brain grey matter comparison between substance users and controls, with a users < controls contrast indicating reduced grey matter in substance users.
- `ADDED` Must be a voxelwise whole brain analysis of grey matter volume
- `ADDED` Multiple experiments from the same article may be included only when they share the same control group but involve non-overlapping substance-user groups. For example, moderate smoker<control, and separately severe smoker<control include two different samples of cigarette smokers.
