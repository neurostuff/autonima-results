# What the criteria revisions actually were

Generated by `scripts/compile_criteria_changes.py`. One row per tier transition on the canonical ladder; `change_type` is a curated reading of the cumulative diff against each source paper's own criteria, verified against the paper's full text where one could be obtained. See `reports/criteria_changes.xlsx` for per-row evidence.

The four categories that track the source — `source-uncovered` (paper states it, the config never encoded it), `source-misencoded` (encoded wrongly), `source-underspecified` (paper vague, operationalised at the same scope) and `source-unenforced` (paper states it but its own included studies violate it) — are all repairs toward the paper. Only `no-source-basis` is fitting to the benchmark with no source argument available.

## The claim this supports

- Of the **5** revisions made before any benchmark output was systematically consulted, **0** had no basis in the source paper. They were corrections of transcription errors and omissions.
- Of the **9** benchmark-tuned revisions, only **3** had no basis in the source paper. The remaining **6** restored criteria the paper states, clarified ones it left vague, or followed criteria it states but does not itself apply.
- **2** of the tuned revisions are `source-unenforced`: the published criteria are contradicted by the published study list. For emotion_regulation_2022 this is directly measured — enforcing its stated criteria faithfully (the archived `v3-allstudies`) collapses full-text recall from 0.840 to 0.404 against the paper's own gold.

Read together: most of what a naive reading would call overfitting is the pipeline being corrected toward the source, not away from it. The irreducible tuning is 3 of 14 revisions across nine projects.
