Inclusion / Exclusion Criteria

Global Criteria

Inclusion

  • GLOBAL_I1: Scope- You are judging ONE analysis, not the paper. The study text is provided only to interpret this analysis. That the paper elsewhere reports a qualifying contrast is not by itself a reason to include this analysis.
  • GLOBAL_I2: How to read the analysis- Judge it from its name, its description and its table caption together. An empty or uninformative analysis name (e.g. "analysis_0") usually means the analysis is the only one in its table, in which case the caption describes it fully and you should judge from the caption. A blank name is never itself a reason to exclude.
  • GLOBAL_I3: The analysis must be a within-group, group-level, whole-brain (voxelwise) task activation contrast.
  • GLOBAL_I4: Sample- Any participant group qualifies, including clinical samples (e.g. schizophrenia, depression) and developmental samples (e.g. adolescents), so long as the contrast is computed WITHIN a group rather than between groups. Do not exclude an analysis because of who the participants were.
  • GLOBAL_I5: Baseline- The comparison condition may be an active control task, or an implicit baseline, rest or fixation. A main effect of task against implicit baseline qualifies. Do not require an active control condition.
  • GLOBAL_I6: Response mode- The task may require an overt behavioral response (e.g. a button press) or a covert one (e.g. silent naming or counting). Do not require an overt response.

Exclusion

  • GLOBAL_E1: Group-comparison contrasts- an analysis whose contrast is between two participant groups (e.g. patients vs controls, adults vs adolescents) or a group x condition interaction. Note this is about the CONTRAST, not the sample: a within-group contrast computed in a patient or adolescent sample is eligible.
  • GLOBAL_E2: Region-of-interest (ROI) or small-volume-corrected (SVC) analyses
  • GLOBAL_E3: Connectivity or resting-state analyses (functional connectivity, PPI, DCM, ICA)
  • GLOBAL_E4: The analysis does not reflect an executive function task contrast at all (e.g. a motor or sensory localiser, a physiological or artefact regressor, or a purely structural result).

Annotation-Specific Criteria (cognitive_flexibility)

Inclusion

  • COGNITIVE_FLEXIBILITY_I1: Task is a FLEXIBILITY paradigm: task switching or the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test
  • COGNITIVE_FLEXIBILITY_I2: The contrast reflects the switching demand (e.g. switch trials > repeat trials, set-shifting > maintenance, or a switching main effect)

Exclusion

  • COGNITIVE_FLEXIBILITY_E1: The contrast being annotated isolates a different executive function domain (e.g., n-back, Go/No-Go, Stroop, Tower tasks, word generation, oddball). Other tasks reported elsewhere in the same paper are irrelevant: judge only the contrast described by this analysis.
